## What FeetFinder Requires From Sellers

### The Age Verification Policy

FeetFinder's Age Verification Policy, effective January 30, 2026, says you "must be at least 18 years old or the age of majority in your jurisdiction (whichever is higher) to create an account, purchase Content, or act as a Seller."

For sellers, it says all sellers must complete a full identity and age verification process, which may include submitting a valid, government-issued photo ID and a selfie or biometric scan for match verification, where permitted by law. Sellers must also confirm that anyone else shown in their content is of legal age and that records are kept.

### Who Sees Your ID

The same policy says FeetFinder works with independent, regulated age-verification providers. It adds: "Where possible, we use providers that return only a 'pass/fail' response and do not share your underlying ID documents with FeetFinder." That's worth knowing if you're nervous about sharing ID. The check is done by a specialist provider, and buyers never see your documents.

### What The Terms Of Service Add

FeetFinder's Terms of Service, effective September 30, 2026, add a few rules that affect verification:

- **Accurate information:** "It is a condition of your use of FeetFinder that all the information you provide on FeetFinder is accurate."
- **Location-based checks:** depending on where you live, "you may be required to verify your age and identity through one or more age verification methods."
- **No false ID:** submitting false identification or using "another person's identity" is "strictly prohibited and may result in immediate account suspension and, where appropriate, referral to law enforcement."
- **Co-creators:** "Each additional depicted person detected by our verification system must complete the joint-account identity and age-verification process before the Content can be posted."

### Verification For Payouts

Passing the age check isn't always the end. The Terms say FeetFinder "may require identity, address, and beneficiary verification (including KYC/KYB/AML checks) before enabling or releasing payouts and on an ongoing basis." It may also withhold or freeze seller earnings, or suspend payout methods, while verification is pending. So make sure your payout details match the identity you verified.

### Tax Forms

FeetFinder's former Seller Agreement asked US sellers for a W-9 and sellers elsewhere for a W-8BEN. That page now redirects to a different agreement, so we treat this as **commonly reported**. Follow whatever forms the sign-up flow asks for. If you're in the US, the IRS explains what [Form W-9](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9) is for. The current Terms say sellers are "solely responsible for their own Tax affairs."

## Why FeetFinder Verification Gets Rejected

FeetFinder doesn't publish a list of rejection reasons. The causes below are **commonly reported** by sellers and verification guides. They're not an official list.

### 1. Unreadable Photos

Blurry or dark images, glare from a flash or window, and cropped corners are the most commonly reported causes. If the provider can't read your name, date of birth and expiry date, the check can fail.

### 2. An Expired Document

Expired IDs are a commonly reported reason for failure. FeetFinder's policy asks for a "valid" ID, so check the expiry date before you upload.

### 3. Details That Don't Match

If the name or date of birth you entered doesn't match your ID, verification may fail. The Terms require accurate information. Use your legal details for verification. Your public seller name can still be a stage name.

### 4. A Poor Selfie Match

If a selfie or biometric scan is requested, sellers report failures when the face is hidden, the lighting is poor or the photo is edited. Take it live, with no filters.

### 5. Location-Based Requirements

The Terms say checks can vary by location. If you're asked for an extra step, complete it. Don't try to get around an age check with a VPN or proxy; it breaks the purpose of verification and can put your account at risk.

### 6. Someone Else In Your Content

If verification has flagged another person in your content, the Terms say they must complete the joint-account process before the content can be posted.

## How To Improve Your Chances

- Use a **valid, unexpired government photo ID**
- Take photos in **soft daylight**, with no flash and no glare
- Keep **all four corners** in frame and make every line readable
- **Match your details** exactly to your ID
- Take selfies **live, with no filters or edits**
- Use a **payout method in your own name**
- Submit **once**, then wait for a reply

## What To Do If Verification Fails

1. **Read the message.** It may say what needs fixing.
2. **Fix the issue.** Retake photos or use a different valid ID.
3. **Resubmit once** through the official site.
4. **Contact support.** FeetFinder's policies give hello@feetfinder.com as the contact address. Include your account email and what you changed.
5. **Don't open a new account.** Creating another account with the same ID is commonly reported to cause more problems.

### Appealing A Decision

If FeetFinder suspends or restricts your account, or withholds earnings, its Appeals Policy, effective September 21, 2026, sets out a process:

- Email **appeals@feetfinder.com**
- Include your account URL or username, the URL of any content involved and "detailed reasons" why you believe you comply with its rules
- File "within six months of the date of the Decision"

The policy says "all appeals undergo a human-led review." FeetFinder can grant, partly grant or deny an appeal. The policy doesn't list a verification failure as an appealable decision, so start with support if your check simply hasn't gone through.

## Beware Of Fake Verification Requests

Scammers target new sellers who are waiting for approval. Commonly reported tricks include:

- A "buyer" or "agent" asking you to pay a fee to get verified
- Messages asking for photos of your ID outside the platform
- Links to lookalike login pages

FeetFinder's policy says verification is done through its own providers. Don't send ID to anyone in messages, and don't pay anyone to "speed up" approval. The FTC's guide on [recognizing and avoiding phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-avoid-phishing-scams) advises contacting a company directly using a website you know is real.
