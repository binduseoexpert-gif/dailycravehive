## The NSFW Tag Is A Warning, Not A Green Light

Ko-fi's content guidelines are direct: "Ko-fi offers the ability to tag your page as NSFW, but this tag does not mean that a Ko-fi page can be used for prohibited content or uses detailed in these guidelines." The "Using the NSFW tag" article is "an extension of" those main guidelines. Its examples use a simple key: some items are "OK with NSFW tag," and some are "Not allowed on Ko-fi (regardless of NSFW tag)." Official help calls that list non-exhaustive. If something is unclear, the same article says to get in touch.

Three basics sit at the top of the tag article:

1. The page must follow Ko-fi's content guidelines even if the NSFW tag is on.
2. If any part of the page contains content that "could be deemed by some audiences as offensive or of a mature nature," tag the page as NSFW. "This must be applied even where the content is locked (i.e. membership tiers and supporters-only content)."
3. If any part of the page **links** to that kind of content, tag the page as NSFW as well.

A locked membership tier does not hide you from the tagging rule, and a link to mature material off the page can still require the tag. The tag still does not make prohibited material allowed.

## How To Apply The NSFW Tag

From Ko-fi's "Using the NSFW tag" article:

1. Log in to your Ko-fi account.
2. Go to **Settings**.
3. Scroll down to the **"What do you do?"** field and add **NSFW** as a tag.

Official help does not describe other official tag names or a tag limit for that field, so this article does not invent them. After you apply the tag, "a pop-up notification will warn visitors to your page that the content on your page may not be safe for work and that they must be 18 or older to view the page." Visitors "will need to click 'Agree and continue' before they can view your page." Keep using the tag "as long as NSFW content is on or linked to your Ko-fi page."

If you do not apply it, official help says Ko-fi "may proactively apply this to your page or ask you to apply it," because "minors might encounter your page." Official help also says: "Although we don't unpublish pages solely because they haven't been tagged as NSFW, we may unpublish such untagged pages that also contain or in some cases are connected to outright prohibited content/activity."

## Adult Creator Offers: What Official Help Does Not Allow

"Adult creator offers" here means selling or promoting adult work (subscriptions, pay-per-view, customs, chat, or similar) on or through a Ko-fi page. Official help treats many of those offers as prohibited, with or without the NSFW tag.

Content Guidelines section 3.14 lists adult-related content, services, and activities Ko-fi does not allow. Official examples of **sexual services** include "prostitution, escorts, pay-per-view, striptease/stripping, masturbation, adult chat/sexting, genital ratings," and "taking and fulfilling requests relating to any prohibited adult content in this list." Official help adds that "sexting includes euphemisms, visual and textual." The same section prohibits **pornography**, including "implied, emulated, censored, and cropped versions," and **sexualised nudity**, including partial exposure of genitalia and/or areolae "in a way intended to be sexually provocative or arousing." Official help recognizes non-sexual nudity in cultural, medical, educational, or artistic contexts and says to contact Ko-fi if you are unsure.

Section 3.14 also prohibits **offering uncensored versions** of sexualised nudity or other prohibited adult content "to subscribers or at third-party sites or links in connection with Ko-fi." Official help may still take action "if we find evidence that money has been received for such content despite an absence of direct URL link(s) to/from Ko-fi." It further prohibits **high-risk fetish and paraphilia content, including findom (financial domination) themes**, and the **sale of other sexually explicit material, including physical items** (official help gives "sex toys" as an example).

The NSFW tag article also marks as not allowed "tip drive content that promises forthcoming sexualised nudity/partial nudity," and content "used to solicit the purchase of or tips in exchange for the full explicit version." Cropping an image does not automatically make it allowed if it is "still identifiable as pornography," including because of an accompanying text description or because Ko-fi's systems "flagged the original pre-cropped image."

Some material is listed as OK with the NSFW tag, such as non-sexualised nudity in cultural, medical, educational, or artistic contexts, and "lightly sensual content featuring clothed side or underboob." Those examples are not a path to adult-creator pay-per-view or fetish sales.

## The Rules Apply Across The Page, DMs And Links

Content Guidelines section 2.1 says the guidelines apply to "all aspects of the Ko-fi service," including imagery, text, video, audio, services, physical items, and links, "located anywhere on Ko-fi including direct messages, galleries, profiles, posts, comments, commissions, shop, and any other location where content can be added to the service."

A commission, shop item, or private message is not a free pass. Official help also reserves the right to enforce its guidelines when the violating content is on an external page linked to or from Ko-fi, and it states: "Ko-fi should not be used to arrange the sale, purchase or transfer of prohibited content via other channels, such as third-party messaging services." This guide will not advise you to disguise a prohibited offer, change labels, or route banned content through another channel.

Creators also have to follow the payment providers Ko-fi uses. Official help says Ko-fi uses [PayPal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal) and [Stripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stripe,_Inc.), and that "creators can't use Ko-fi in ways that go against PayPal's acceptable use policy or Stripe's prohibited businesses terms." If payments come through Ko-fi "for something not allowed by your payment provider," official help says Ko-fi might unpublish the page temporarily or, "in more serious or repeated cases, permanently." Those provider policies are separate documents, named here rather than quoted.
