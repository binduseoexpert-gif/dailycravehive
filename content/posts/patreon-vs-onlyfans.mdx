## Content Rules: The Deciding Factor

### What Patreon Allows

Patreon's Community Guidelines say the platform "permits a broad range of expression and creative works," including "works featuring sexual, sensual, and suggestive themes," restricted to paying members who've attested that they're over 18. The same section is blunt: "Patreon is not a place for sexual activity by or between real and hyperrealistic subjects."

Under the Adult/18+ rules:

- **Not permitted:** works depicting sexual activity between real participants. This includes masturbation, stimulation of the genitals, and "simulating sexual activity using body parts (e.g. tongue, mouth, or feet) or objects." Close-ups focused solely on nude genitals and depictions of sexualized bodily fluids are also banned.
- **Permitted only on Adult/18+ pages:** real human nudity that doesn't cross those lines; "works that primarily depict clear kink and fetish, without featuring sexual activity"; and erotic writing or audio.
- **Paywall rule:** adult works must sit behind the subscription paywall. Public parts of your page (profile image, banner, tier descriptions, free posts) "must be free of nudity."
- **Age and consent:** Adult/18+ creators must complete age verification and get **written consent** from every real adult who appears in their work. Patreon provides a consent form for this.

**What this means for feet creators:** non-sexual feet content and "clear kink and fetish" content can fit on a Patreon Adult/18+ page. Content where feet are used to simulate sexual activity is explicitly not permitted. If your content goes there, Patreon isn't the right platform.

Patreon's guidelines also restrict steering fans off the platform. They say attempts to "direct, coerce, lure, or otherwise lead people from Patreon to third party websites or experiences are strictly prohibited unless otherwise stated." Read Patreon's Help Center on permitted off-platform activity before promoting another page from Patreon.

### What OnlyFans Allows

OnlyFans was built for adults. Its Creator Center says the platform is only available to people 18 or older, and every creator must pass identity verification with a government ID and a selfie. Adult content is permitted, but OnlyFans still removes content that breaks its rules. Its Creator Center lists the most common reasons, which include:

- Content featuring someone whose age can't be verified
- Sexually explicit content without all participants tagged and verified
- Nudity in public places where the general public is present

## Fees Compared

### Patreon Fees

[Patreon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patreon) is a membership platform. Its pricing page says it is "free to start" and charges **10% of the income you earn on Patreon**, plus payment processing, currency conversion, payout fees and applicable taxes. The Help Center explains the details:

- **Platform fee:** creators who published their page after August 4, 2025 are on the standard 10% plan. Older creators may keep a legacy plan of 5%, 8% or 11%. If you unpublish your page, or Patreon unpublishes it, you move to the 10% plan when you republish.
- **Payment processing:** charged on top. For USD payouts, Patreon lists 2.9% + $0.30 for cards, Apple Pay and US PayPal/Venmo, and 3.9% + $0.30 for non-US PayPal/Venmo. Other currencies have their own rates.
- **Currency conversion:** 2.5% when a fan pays in a currency different from your payout currency.
- **iOS App Store fee:** for purchases made in the Patreon iOS app, Apple takes a 30% fee. Patreon says this replaces its standard processing fee for that transaction.
- **Payout fee:** a fee for transferring your balance. It depends on your payout method and currency.

**Two places where Patreon's own pages don't match:**

- **Small tiers.** The "Creator fees overview" says the standard 10% plan "applies the same processing rates to all payments, regardless of amount." The same article's fee factors, and Patreon's August 2025 standard-plan article, still say tiers of $3 or less get micropayment rates. If you plan a tier at $3 or under, check the rate shown in your own Plan details.
- **One-time sales.** The "Commerce pricing, fees, and taxes" article says the platform fee on Commerce digital sales is 5%. The "Selling one-time purchases" guide lists a standard 10% fee, with legacy rates for creators who started selling before August 4, 2025. The "Creator fees overview" says between 5% and 12%, depending on your plan. Treat 5% as possible for some accounts, not a guarantee.

## Payouts Compared

**Patreon:** its Subscription Billing FAQ says you can "receive an automatic payout on the 5th or manually withdraw funds anytime." The "Paying out your earnings" article adds that you can only start a payout once every 24 hours, and a 5-day payout lock applies whenever you add or edit a payout method. On timing, Patreon's pages differ again: one says deposits "typically arrive within 10 days, depending on your payout method," while the automatic payouts article says a transfer "can take 3-5 business days" to reach PayPal or a bank. Product sales are slower to clear: Commerce funds can be pending for up to 7 days, and iOS product sales for up to 75 days.

**OnlyFans:** the Creator Center says earnings first go into a pending balance. A tip received on June 1 stays pending "until at least June 8th." Cleared funds can be withdrawn manually or on a weekly or monthly schedule. Payouts take "between three to five business days, on average." Payout options and timing vary by country.

## Patreon's November 1, 2026 Billing Change

This is the biggest Patreon change of the year, and the deadline is days away as we write. Patreon's Help Center says: "All creators still on legacy billing must switch to subscription billing by November 1, 2026." Legacy billing means first-of-the-month billing or per-creation billing.

Key points from Patreon's official articles:

- **If you don't switch, Patreon switches you.** Patreon says it will "pause your billing and migrate you using a default multiplier." Billing stays paused until you log in and set or confirm your prices. For per-creation creators who didn't contact Support by October 15, 2026, Patreon says the automatic switch happens "in November" and it will email you once it's done.
- **Per-creation default prices:** each tier's price is multiplied by the highest number of paid posts you charged for in a single month over the last 3 months. In Patreon's example, a $5 per-creation tier with up to 3 paid posts a month becomes $15 a month.
- **Paused months aren't charged later.** Fans won't be billed for the time your billing was paused.
- **Existing members** keep being charged on the 1st of the month. **New members** are charged when they join and then monthly on that date.
- **It's permanent.** Patreon says you can't revert after switching.
- **Payout schedule doesn't change.**

Patreon's deadlines for one-to-one migration help have all passed: September 30, 2026 for first-of-the-month creators, October 1 for iOS-related help and October 15 for per-creation creators. If you're on first-of-the-month billing, you can still switch yourself under Settings, then Billing and payouts. If you're on legacy billing and reading this after November 1, check your Billing and payouts settings for a pause and confirm your new prices so fans can be charged again. OnlyFans has no equivalent change.

## Taxes On Either Platform

Income from Patreon and OnlyFans is generally taxable either way. In the US, platforms may send an information return such as Form 1099-NEC or 1099-K. The IRS page "[Am I required to file a Form 1099 or other information return?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/am-i-required-to-file-a-form-1099-or-other-information-return)" says the Form 1099-NEC reporting threshold is **$2,000 for payments made in 2026** ($600 for payments before 2026). Income is taxable even if you never receive a form. Our [OnlyFans taxes guide](/onlyfans-taxes) covers self-employment tax, estimated payments and record-keeping. This isn't tax advice.
