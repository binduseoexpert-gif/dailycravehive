A subscriber messages you: they paid, they're sure they're logged in, and they still can't see your posts. Or they can see the post, but the photos and videos stay locked. On Fansly, that is often a **permissions** issue, not a broken subscription.

Two settings work separately:

- **Timeline permissions** control who can see the posts on your feed.
- **Media permissions** control who can unlock the images, videos or voice notes on a given post or DM.

A subscription does **not** automatically include media you priced separately, limited to another tier, or locked behind a tip. Fansly's own subscriber-facing help says "not all content may be visible immediately" and that "this doesn't always mean something is wrong."

**Don't "fix" it by making everything public.** Opening the whole timeline to everyone, or stripping every media lock, can give access you didn't mean to give. Check the intended offer first, then adjust only the setting that's wrong.

## Timeline Access Is Not The Same As Media Access

Fansly's "Permissions on Fansly" article says: "Timeline permissions control who can view the content you post on your timeline." Those settings "don't affect public profile elements like your username, profile photo, subscription tiers, or bio."

"Manage Who Can See My Posts" repeats that your username, profile photo, bio and subscription tiers are always visible, even with a limited timeline.

Media is a second layer. When you add images, videos or voice notes to a post or DM, you see **Limit who can see this**. You can tick Follow you, Subscribe or Pay, or click **Advanced** for things like different prices for different tiers or date limits. Creator Hub puts it simply: you can set prices for access "or restrict viewing to certain subscriber tiers."

So a fan can be allowed to *see that a post exists* and still need to meet that post's media rules to open the file. That's expected when you sell pay-per-view or tiered media.

## Step 1: Check Timeline Permissions

Fansly's creator-facing troubleshooting article says: "Make sure your timeline permissions are set up correctly," and links to your privacy settings. Creator Hub lists the same area as **Settings > Privacy & Safety**.

From "Manage Who Can See My Posts":

1. Go to your **Privacy Settings**.
2. Scroll down to **Timeline Permissions**.
3. Under **Limit who can see your posts**, choose what fans must do: Follow you, Subscribe, Have tipped you, or Followed by you (only people you follow).
4. Check **Fans will see** to confirm the setup.

You can tick more than one option, and **"Fans must meet all of them."** For more combinations, click **Advanced**.

Fansly also notes: "Leave everything unticked to keep your timeline open to everyone." That describes the open default. It is not a troubleshooting step. If your offer is subscriber-only, leaving everything unticked would *create* a mismatch, because it opens the feed to people who aren't paying.

### A Common Timeline Conflict

Fansly's own example: "if your follow button is turned off, but following is a requirement to view your timeline, new users won't be able to see your content."

Creator Hub says you can disable the follow button under **Creator Dashboard > Plans/Promos**, where you can also switch on "Require Payment Method to Follow." If following is part of your timeline rule, make sure people can actually follow you. A stacked rule like "Follow you + Subscribe" means a subscriber who can't follow is still locked out.

### Location Blocks

Creator Hub says that in Privacy & Safety "you can block entire cities, states or countries from viewing your profile." A subscriber in a blocked location won't see the page the way you do. Check that before you touch any permissions.

### FYP Vs The Timeline

If the timeline is limited, Fansly says you can turn on **"Internal discovery when the timeline is hidden"** so your posts can still be recommended on the For You Page. That's discovery, not a substitute for giving a subscriber feed access. Promotion habits live in our [how to promote Fansly](/how-to-promote-fansly) guide.

## Step 2: Check That Post's Media Permissions

If the subscriber can see the post but the media is locked, look at the **files**, not only the timeline.

Fansly's subscriber help says: "Some creators require more than just an active subscription. Certain posts may also need an additional purchase. Select the Lock icon on the post to see what's required to unlock the content."

On the creator side, "Permissions on Fansly" says to click the **three blue dots** in the top-right corner of the post, then select **Media Permissions**.

The creator troubleshooting article is direct: "Ensure that their subscription tier is included in the permissions for the media."

Fansly's rule of thumb inside Advanced: conditions in the **same box** must all be met, while separate boxes are alternative ways in. The **Fans will see** line at the bottom sums up the setup in words. Read it before you post.

If your subscriber is on Tier 1 and the media is set to a VIP tier only, the lock is working as designed. Either add their tier, or explain that the post isn't part of their plan.

### Don't Open Every Lock As The First Fix

You *can* change media permissions after posting. Fansly says fans who already **purchased** the media keep access, and everyone else must meet the new requirements. That is not a reason to drop every lock in your library. Change the one post (or saved audience) that doesn't match what you sold.

Creator Hub's pro tip: click the three dots on your profile page or next to a media post and select **"View Media As"** to preview how different users see your content. Do that before you reply to the complaint.

### Check Your Saved Audiences

If you reuse permission setups with **Save as audience**, one wrong saved audience can repeat the same lock across many posts. If several subscribers report the same problem on recent posts, check the saved audience you've been applying, not only the single post.

## Purchased Media Vs Tipped Media

Fansly calls this a "key difference":

- **Purchased media** "stays accessible to the user, even if you change the permissions or delete your account."
- **Tipped for media** "may become unavailable if you update the permissions or delete the content after it's been unlocked."

If someone paid a set unlock price, they should keep that file after a permission change. If they unlocked it through a tip condition, a later permission edit can take it away. Don't promise "once unlocked, always unlocked" for tip-gated files. If a fan should keep something for good, a priced unlock fits that promise better than a tip threshold.

## What To Tell The Subscriber

Keep the reply short and factual:

1. Confirm they're logged into the account that holds the subscription, and on the tier they think they're on.
2. Ask whether they can't see the post at all, or only see a lock on the media.
3. If the media is pay-per-view or for another tier, say so and point them to the lock icon, which Fansly says shows what's required.
4. Fansly's subscriber help also suggests checking the creator's profile for notes or instructions on how to access exclusive content. If your offer has rules, put them there.
5. If your settings look correct, ask them to contact Fansly Support.

A subscription to your page is not a promise that every file is included. It works like a paid membership with separately priced extras, and saying that clearly up front heads off most of these messages.

## When It's Not Permissions

Fansly's subscriber article lists other ordinary explanations:

- **"This user has not posted anything yet"** means there are no posts, not a failed subscription.
- Content may simply not have been uploaded yet.

Live streams are a **separate** control, not the same as timeline or media permissions. Fansly's "Monetizing Your Live Streams" article uses a **Limit who can watch** checklist: Follow you, Subscribe, Buy a ticket, or Have tipped you. You can tick more than one, fans must meet all of them, and **Advanced** lets fans in through a private list. Official wording: **"If nothing is ticked, everyone can watch."** Don't confuse a stream access problem with a recorded-post problem, and don't untick every live-stream box to clear a complaint about a feed post. Stream setup and key security are in our guide on [how to go live on Fansly](/fansly-live-stream).

## Final Thoughts

When a Fansly subscriber can't see your content, start with two official checks: timeline permissions, and the media permissions on the post they're asking about. A paid subscription is not a master key for separately priced or other-tier files, and opening everything to the public is the wrong first move. Match the settings to the offer you actually sold, preview with View Media As, and send stubborn cases to Fansly Support.
