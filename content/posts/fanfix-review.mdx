## What Is Fanfix?

The Fanfix FAQ describes it as "a subscription-based platform that helps creators monetize exclusive, members-only content in a safe-for-work, brand-friendly environment." Fans subscribe monthly to a creator's page. Creators can also earn through direct messages, locked content sent in message blasts and other "Revenue Programs" the Terms describe.

The FAQ also lists the features creators get:

- Subscription tiers
- Direct messages and mass messages, which can be free or paid and sent to custom lists
- Scheduled messages
- Live streaming
- Insights and analytics for earnings by media type
- Creator safety features the FAQ calls Creator Moderation, Payment Protection and Watermarks

Fanfix's FAQ claims a community of "over 15 million creators, entertainers, entrepreneurs, and fans" and says top creators earn "over $1 million monthly." These are the platform's own marketing claims. We can't verify them independently, and they say nothing about what a typical new creator earns.

## Is Fanfix Legit?

Based on its official documents, Fanfix looks like a real, structured business. It isn't a fly-by-night operation. It publishes full Terms of Service, separate Creator and Payment Terms and detailed Content Guidelines. It processes creator payouts through Stripe, a large, well-known payment company ([Stripe on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stripe,_Inc.)). The Creator Terms say Fanfix may ask creators to verify their age and identity through Persona Identities, Inc., a third-party verification provider.

"Legit" doesn't mean "right for everyone," though. A few clauses are worth knowing before you apply:

- **Arbitration.** The General Terms contain an arbitration provision (Section 9). Disputes go to binding arbitration rather than court, and class actions and jury trials are not permitted.
- **Payment withholding.** The Creator Terms say Fanfix may withhold, deduct or recover creator fees, "whether or not yet paid out," if it believes you have or may have breached the terms.
- **Negative balances.** Refunds can push an account balance into the negative. Fanfix reserves the right to recover that from future payments.
- **Termination.** Fanfix can suspend or terminate access if you break the agreement.

None of this is unusual for creator platforms. Still, read the Terms yourself rather than relying on a summary, including this one.

Also watch out for impersonators. Fake "talent scouts" and fake support accounts target creators on every platform. Fanfix's own FAQ says referrals go through its Creator Success team, so be wary of strangers who promise guaranteed approval. The FTC's guide to [recognizing and avoiding phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-avoid-phishing-scams) is a good refresher. ## Fanfix Fees: The 80/20 Split

Section 3.1 of the Creator Terms says creators receive **80% of direct payments** made by fans for memberships, direct messages, locked content and other Revenue Programs, unless other program rules say otherwise. Section 3.2 says Fanfix keeps **the remaining 20%**. That 20% doesn't include taxes, which are the creator's own responsibility.

That puts Fanfix's headline split in line with other major subscription platforms. For comparison, see our breakdown of [OnlyFans fees and payouts](/onlyfans-fees-payouts).

### The 13% Card Surcharge (Fan Side)

Section 1.5 of Fanfix's Payment Terms says: "We impose a surcharge of 13% on all credit cards." This is charged to the paying fan, so it doesn't come out of your 80%. It does matter, though, because it raises what fans pay for your content. A $10 subscription costs a fan more than $10 when they pay by credit card.

The Creator Terms also note that some banks may charge fans a foreign transaction fee, "typically around 3.0%," which Fanfix doesn't control.

### Direct Message Pricing

Per the Creator Terms, Fanfix sets the minimum price for paid direct messages "in our sole discretion." Creators can ask for adjustments, and Fanfix may accept or reject them. If you plan to rely on paid DMs, you won't have full control over the price floor.

### Refunds For Fans

The Payment Terms say membership fees are non-refundable and aren't pro-rated if a fan cancels mid-term. The fan keeps access until the end of the current term. Fanfix reserves the right to issue refunds or discounts at its discretion, and the Creator Terms say Fanfix handles chargebacks and payment disputes.

## Fanfix Payouts: How And When You Get Paid

According to the official FAQ:

- **Processor:** all creator payouts go through Stripe. You set up a Stripe account during onboarding, and it holds your transactions, payout analytics and tax forms.
- **Who can use Stripe payouts:** "all US and UK based creators can only receive payouts through Stripe." Creators elsewhere are told to contact support to review options. If you're outside the US or UK, confirm this before you put work into a page.
- **Schedule:** earnings are "processed every Wednesday" and "typically deposited every Friday." Some international regions take longer (3–7 days), but processing is still weekly. The FAQ says creators can adjust the day and interval of payouts.
- **Updating bank details:** done inside Stripe, reached from Settings, then Transaction, then "Go To Stripe."

The FAQ lists the most common reasons for delayed payouts: problems with banking details, and needing to update an SSN or business tax information in Stripe.

We didn't find a stated minimum payout amount on the official pages we checked. Some third-party sites mention one, but treat any specific figure as **commonly reported** and check inside your Stripe dashboard.

### Taxes

Fanfix's Creator Terms say creators are "solely responsible" for reporting and paying their taxes, and that Fanfix may withhold sums it's legally required to withhold. In the US, creator income is generally self-employment income. The IRS [Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) is the best starting point. This isn't tax advice, so speak to a qualified professional.

## Content Rules: What "Safe-For-Work" Means

This is the section most readers of this site need to understand.

Fanfix's FAQ calls the platform "strictly safe-for-work (SFW)." The General Terms include a **"No Nudity"** rule. It bans photos, videos and digitally-created content showing sexual intercourse, genitals or "close-ups of fully-nude buttocks." That includes face-swapped or manipulated images. The Content Guidelines also prohibit offering sexual services.

The FAQ lists the kinds of content that work on Fanfix: behind-the-scenes posts, exclusive photos and videos, early-access drops, personal updates, Q&As and creative projects.

**What about feet content?** Fanfix's official pages we read don't specifically mention feet content one way or the other. Non-nude photos may technically fall within the rules, but Fanfix positions itself as "brand-friendly," and its Guidelines give the platform broad discretion to remove or limit content. ### No Promoting Other Platforms

This clause catches many creators out. Section 2.5 of the Creator Terms says you must not use your Fanfix account to promote "Competitive Platforms." It names Patreon, Passes and OnlyFans, and includes "any other online content monetization platforms." Creator fees earned in breach of this can be withheld or recovered. If your strategy is to funnel Fanfix fans to an adult page, Fanfix's own Terms rule that out.


## How To Join Fanfix

The FAQ describes Fanfix as **invite-only**. Every application, whether direct or referred, is reviewed by Fanfix's Partnerships team. Applicants "must include valid and active social media handles." Applications without verified social profiles "will not be reviewed."

This matters if you're starting from scratch. Fanfix is built for creators who already have a following they can convert. If you have no audience yet, our guide to [starting with no followers](/start-onlyfans-no-followers) covers audience-building basics that apply on any platform.

Once accepted, Fanfix may send you to Persona for age and identity verification. According to the FAQ, onboarding includes setting up Stripe, "dedicated onboarding support" and "personalized launch guidance."
