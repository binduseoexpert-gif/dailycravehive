## How Much Does OnlyFans Take?

OnlyFans takes **20%**. Its Terms of Service, last updated August 2024 according to the page, say: "Our Fee is calculated as 20% of the total Fan Payment and will be deducted from each Fan Payment."

The creator sign-up page puts it the other way round: "OnlyFans creators own 100% of their content, and keep 80% of all earnings."

### What The 20% Covers

The fee applies to fan payments, not just subscriptions. Subscriptions, tips, pay-per-view (PPV) messages and posts, and paid streams all count as fan payments. OnlyFans doesn't charge creators a separate sign-up or monthly fee to open an account. Your cost is the share of what fans pay you.

### Other Things That Can Reduce Your Earnings

- **Refunds and chargebacks.** The Terms say that if a fan "successfully seeks a refund or chargeback," OnlyFans "may deduct an amount equal to the Creator Earnings portion" from your balance.
- **Bank and conversion charges.** The Terms say OnlyFans isn't responsible for banking, currency exchange or e-wallet charges.
- **VAT and local taxes.** Depending on where you and your fans live, tax may apply. OnlyFans publishes a separate UK VAT Policy. Get tax advice for your own country.

## The Pending Balance: Why Your Money Isn't Available Immediately

Every sale first lands in your **pending balance**, not in money you can withdraw. The Creator Center explains: "All of your OnlyFans earnings – whether they come from subscriptions, tips, PPVs, or anything else – are first added to your pending balance."

### Is It Really 7 Days?

OnlyFans doesn't state a fixed number of days on the pages we checked. Its own example does show the timing, though: "if a fan tips you on June 1st, that money will be held in your pending balance until at least June 8th before entering your current balance."

So **about seven days is the minimum hold**, going by OnlyFans' own example. Note the words "at least." The hold can be longer.

### The 21-Day Rule For New Creators

Creator forums and third-party guides commonly report that new accounts wait longer, often 21 days, for their first earnings to clear. We **couldn't find this on OnlyFans' Terms or Creator Center**, so we can't confirm it. Treat it as a possibility, not a rule, and check the Statements page in your own account.

### Current Balance

Once funds leave the pending balance, they move to your **current balance**. The Creator Center says: "As soon as funds have cleared your pending balance and entered your current balance, you can withdraw."

## The OnlyFans Payout Minimum

The Terms confirm a minimum exists: "To withdraw Creator Earnings from your OnlyFans account, your account balance must meet the minimum payout amount requirement."

They don't give the amount. A **$20 minimum is commonly reported**, but we couldn't find that figure on the official pages we checked. The amount may also vary by payout method or country. Check the Banking or Statements section of your account for the minimum that applies to you.

In practice, the minimum rarely holds up an active creator. It matters most in your first few weeks, when your current balance is small and some funds may still be pending.

## How And When You Get Paid

### Manual Or Automatic Withdrawals

According to the Creator Center, you can "always choose to manually withdraw funds" from your balance. Many creators set up **weekly or monthly automatic withdrawals** instead. Any pending funds that reach your current balance "by the automatic withdrawal date specified in your 'Statements' page will be included in that payout."

Here's a simple way to choose:

- **Manual** gives you control. Withdraw when you need the money, as long as your current balance meets the minimum.
- **Weekly automatic** gives you a steady, predictable income without remembering to request each payout.
- **Monthly automatic** means fewer, larger transfers. That can help if your bank charges per transfer.

### How Long Payouts Take

The Creator Center says: "All payouts, manual or automatic, will take between three to five business days, on average, to process into your bank account." You can track each request in the Payout Requests section of your Statements.

OnlyFans also says that "the withdrawal options available to you and the time it takes for your earnings to arrive can vary depending on where you live." If you're outside the US or UK, expect your timing to differ. **Check the official site** and your Banking section for the options in your country.

### Changing Your Payout Method

You can choose a new payout method from the Banking section of your account, according to the Creator Center.

## From Fan Payment To Your Bank Account: A Timeline

Using OnlyFans' published information, a typical payout looks like this:

1. **Day 0:** A fan pays you $25. OnlyFans deducts 20%, and $20 goes into your pending balance.
2. **Day 7 or later:** The money moves to your current balance. New accounts may wait longer, which is commonly reported but not confirmed.
3. **Withdrawal:** You request a payout, or your automatic withdrawal date arrives. Your current balance has to meet the minimum.
4. **3–5 business days later, on average:** The money reaches your bank account, depending on your country and method.

So money from a sale on the 1st might not reach your bank until around the middle of the month. Plan your budget around that gap, especially in your first month.

## OnlyFans Terms Changes From 1 November 2026

OnlyFans has rewritten its Terms of Service, and creators are being asked to accept the new version at login. As of 22 October 2026, the main public terms page still shows the August 2024 version, so the changes below are as shown to creators at login and reported in the press (including the St. Louis Riverfront Times on 6 October 2026). They are due to apply from 1 November 2026.

What changes for your money:

- **Easier earnings holds.** OnlyFans can suspend an account and hold earnings if it believes a creator "has or may have breached" the terms. The 2024 wording required a "serious or repeated" breach.
- **Two new suspension triggers.** Any action OnlyFans believes is reasonably likely to cause a loss, or harm to anyone's reputation, and not promptly providing information OnlyFans asks for.
- **Currency-conversion fees on payouts.** Payouts that need a currency conversion now include conversion fees. The old text only warned that your own bank might charge you.
- **Disputes in writing.** You still have six months to dispute a suspension, deletion or held earnings, but the dispute now has to be in writing.
- **Affiliate links count as advertising.** Affiliate links you post now need to be labelled as ads.

What doesn't change: **the 20% fee.** Creators still keep 80% of fan payments.

In practice, answer OnlyFans emails and information requests quickly, keep written records, and expect a little less on payouts if your bank account is in a different currency.

## Tips For Keeping More Of What You Earn

You can't negotiate the 20% fee, but you can control the rest:

- **Price with the fee in mind.** If you want $8 from a subscription, a $10 price covers the fee.
- **Avoid chargebacks.** Describe your PPV content clearly so fans get what they paid for.
- **Compare your bank's fees.** A cheaper payout method can save a few dollars on every withdrawal.
- **Put money aside for tax.** The Terms make tax your responsibility. Our guide to [taxes on OnlyFans income](/onlyfans-taxes) explains self-employment tax, 1099 forms and quarterly payments.
- **Use automatic withdrawals** so cleared money doesn't sit in your account.
