## What Instagram's Rules Actually Say

### Nudity

Instagram's Community Guidelines say "we don't allow nudity on Instagram." The rule covers photos, videos and some digitally created content showing "sexual intercourse, genitals, and close-ups of fully-nude buttocks," plus some photos of female nipples, with exceptions for contexts such as breastfeeding, health-related situations and protest. Nudity in photos of paintings and sculptures is allowed.

Feet aren't on that list. A clean, well-lit photo of feet in sandals on a beach isn't nudity under the published rule. The problem usually isn't the photo — it's **what you say next to it**.

### Sexual Solicitation

Meta's "Adult Sexual Solicitation and Sexually Explicit Language" policy is the one that matters most for sellers. Meta says it draws the line "when content facilitates sexual encounters or commercial sexual services between adults or when content asks for or offers pornographic or sexual content."

The policy lists solicitation Meta doesn't allow, including offering, asking for or providing contact methods for:

- "Sexual Fetishes"
- "Sex chats or conversations"
- "Sharing of nude imagery (such as nudes, sex videos)"

It also bans "content that asks for, offers or provides methods of contact to acquire pornographic material, or contains usernames or links to pornographic websites," and "sexual solicitation combined with a price or any signal of a transaction for money or something of value." Separately, the policy restricts sexually explicit language about states of arousal and sexual encounters.

Put together: a caption like "foot fetish content, DM for prices" combines a fetish offer with a transaction signal. That's the pattern the policy describes.

### Adult Subscription Links

Meta's policy has a separate line for adult subscription sites. It says Meta limits viewing to adults 18 and older for "content that contains usernames, links to or logos of Adult Subscription Websites." In the policy wording, that's an age restriction rather than an outright ban — but it shrinks who can see the post.

## A Safer Instagram Funnel

Think of Instagram as the shop window. The checkout lives elsewhere.

### Step 1: Separate Identity

Make a creator account that isn't tied to your personal profile. Don't reuse your personal email, phone or photos that show your face, home or tattoos. Turn off contact syncing so the account isn't suggested to people you know.

### Step 2: Post SFW Content Only

Style shots, pedicures, shoes, sand, socks, nail art. Keep it something you'd be comfortable seeing in a fashion or beauty feed. Avoid captions that describe arousal or fetish acts — Meta's policy restricts sexually explicit language separately from images.

### Step 3: Keep Captions Neutral

Describe the photo, not the sale. "Fresh pedicure" is fine. "Selling feet pics" in a caption is the sort of offer Meta's policy describes.

## Scams That Target Instagram Feet Pages

Feet sellers on Instagram see the same scripts again and again (**commonly reported**):

- **"Pay first, I'll send after"** buyers who send a fake payment screenshot
- **Overpayment** — "I sent too much, refund the difference"
- **Fake verification** — "Pay $X to verify you're a real seller"
- **"Agency" or "manager"** DMs that want your login or a monthly fee
- **Phishing** links pretending to be Instagram support

Treat anyone asking for your password in a DM as a scammer, whatever badge or name they use. Report and block.
