## Is OnlyFans Or Feet Pic Income Taxable?

Yes. The IRS's [Gig Economy Tax Center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gig-economy-tax-center) says you must report gig economy income on a tax return "even if the income is":

- "From part-time, temporary or side work"
- "Not reported on an information return form — like a Form 1099-K, 1099-MISC, 1099-NEC, W-2 or other income statement"
- "Paid in any form, including cash, property, goods, or virtual currency"

The IRS's list of gig work includes selling goods online and providing "creative or professional services," which is close to what creators do. So the common belief that you don't owe tax unless you get a 1099 is wrong. You owe tax on taxable income whether or not a form arrives.

That includes tips, pay-per-view messages, custom requests and referral earnings, as well as subscriptions. If it came to you through your creator business, treat it as income and ask a professional if you're unsure.

## Self-Employment Tax: The Part That Surprises People

Most creators are independent contractors, not employees, so nobody withholds tax from their payouts. That's where self-employment tax comes in.

According to the IRS page on [self-employment tax](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes):

- "The self-employment tax rate is 15.3%," made up of "12.4% for social security" and "2.9% for Medicare."
- You must pay self-employment tax and file Schedule SE if "your net earnings from self-employment... were $400 or more."
- You can deduct the employer-equivalent portion of your SE tax when working out your adjusted gross income.
- The rules apply "no matter how old you are."

The same page says the Social Security part only applies up to a yearly earnings cap, and that higher earners may owe an additional 0.9% Medicare tax above certain thresholds (for example, $200,000 for single filers). Most new creators won't hit those limits, but bigger earners should know they exist.

Self-employment tax is **on top of** regular income tax. That's why it's commonly recommended that creators set aside a share of every payout for tax. Some creator guides suggest a fixed percentage, but there's no official figure that fits everyone. The right amount depends on your income, expenses and where you live, so ask a tax professional.

### Form 1099-NEC And The New $2,000 Threshold

The IRS page "[Am I required to file a Form 1099 or other information return?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/am-i-required-to-file-a-form-1099-or-other-information-return)" says: "For payments made before 2026, the reporting threshold is $600. For payments made in 2026, the reporting threshold is $2,000." For payments after 2026, the IRS says to check Publication 1099 for the inflation-adjusted threshold.

What this means for creators:

- For **2025 income**, filed in early 2026, the old $600 threshold applied
- For **2026 income**, filed in early 2027, a payer generally only has to issue a 1099-NEC once payments reach the $2,000 threshold
- Either way, **you still report all your income**, including amounts below the threshold

**Conflicting figures:** the $600 figure that's **commonly reported** in OnlyFans' Tax Policy matches the old IRS rule, not the $2,000 threshold for 2026 payments. The IRS figure is the official one. A platform can still choose to send you a form below the threshold, and it doesn't change what you owe.

### Form 1099-K

Some payments go through payment apps or online marketplaces. The IRS page [Understanding your Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k) says these must send one "if the payments you received for goods or services total over $20,000 in more than 200 transactions. However, they may send you a Form 1099-K with lower amounts and/or transactions." If you use several platforms, you could get more than one.

Which form you get, if any, depends on how a platform pays you. Don't count the same income twice if it appears on more than one form, and keep records so you can reconcile them.

### Where To Find Your Forms

- **OnlyFans:** **commonly reported** to be in the Banking or Statements area of your account
- **Other platforms:** check your account's tax or payout settings, and your email (including spam) in January. The IRS says payment apps and marketplaces must send your 1099-K copy by January 31.

## Estimated Taxes

Because nothing is withheld, the IRS says self-employed people "may have to file Estimated Taxes quarterly." According to its estimated tax FAQ, you generally have to make estimated payments if you expect to owe at least $1,000 in tax for the year after withholding and refundable credits, and your withholding and credits will be less than the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000).

## Deductions: What Creators Commonly Claim

Self-employed people generally report business income and expenses on Schedule C, and the IRS says sole proprietors and independent contractors generally use Schedule C to work out net earnings from self-employment. That net figure is what self-employment tax is based on, so keeping track of real business costs matters.

Creators **commonly report** claiming ordinary and necessary business costs, such as:

- Platform plans or subscription fees (for example, a FeetFinder seller plan)
- Equipment such as cameras, lighting and phone accessories, depending on how they're used
- Props, outfits and nail or foot-care products used only for content
- Editing software and apps
- Part of your phone and internet bill used for the business
- Paid promotion within platform rules

Whether something counts as a deduction depends on the rules and your facts. Keep receipts, and check with a tax professional before claiming anything. Platform fees are usually taken before payout, so check whether your income figure is gross or net, and don't deduct a fee you were never paid in the first place.

## Records To Keep

Good records make filing easier and help if anything is ever questioned:

- Monthly earnings statements from each platform
- Tax forms (1099-NEC, 1099-K) and the W-9 or W-8BEN you submitted
- Receipts for every business expense
- Bank records of payouts
- Records of any estimated tax payments

## If You're Outside The US

Rules differ from country to country. OnlyFans' Terms say creators must report income "to the relevant Tax authority in your jurisdiction." Creators registered for UK VAT must follow OnlyFans' UK VAT Policy, and creators registered in an EU country must follow the EU VAT rules that apply. Non-US creators are **commonly reported** to be asked for a W-8BEN form instead of a W-9. Check your own tax authority's guidance and ask a local professional.
