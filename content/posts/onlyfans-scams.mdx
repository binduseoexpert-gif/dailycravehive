## Why Scammers Target OnlyFans Creators

Creators have an account with money in it, a public brand and often a strong wish to grow. That makes them a target for two kinds of scam: one that gets into your account, and one that gets a share of your income by promising growth.

The stakes are high. OnlyFans' Terms of Service say OnlyFans isn't responsible for "any lost, stolen, or compromised User accounts, passwords, email accounts, or any resulting unauthorised activities or resulting unauthorised payments or withdrawals of funds." In plain terms, keeping your login safe is your job.

## 1. Fake Management Agencies

### How It Works

You get a DM or email from an "agency" that promises to grow your page. Common pitches, as reported by creators, include:

- Guaranteed income figures ("$10k in your first month")
- A "free trial" that requires your login details
- An upfront "onboarding," "marketing" or "setup" fee
- A contract with a large share of your earnings, a long lock-in period or ownership of your content

Some agencies are legitimate businesses. The problem is that from the outside, a real one and a fake one can look almost identical.

### Red Flags

- **They want your password.** A business that asks for your login gets full control of your page, your messages with fans and possibly your payout settings. If something goes wrong, OnlyFans' Terms say it isn't responsible for compromised accounts or unauthorised withdrawals.
- **Guaranteed earnings.** No one can guarantee what fans will spend.
- **Upfront fees.** Scam agencies commonly ask for money before doing any work.
- **Pressure to sign quickly,** or contracts that claim your content, your account or your social media.
- **No verifiable history.** No real website, no reachable references, or "proof" that is only screenshots.

### How To Protect Yourself

- Never give your login to anyone you haven't fully checked, and think hard before giving it to anyone at all.
- Read any contract in full. Look at the percentage, the length, how you can leave, and who owns your content and accounts.
- Ask for references from current creators and contact them yourself.
- Don't pay an upfront fee for promised growth.
- Keep control of your email address, payout method and identity documents.

OnlyFans also says that "to ensure that profiles remain in the control of their verified creators, all accounts are subject to 'selfie checks' on a regular basis," according to its Creator Center. An agency that tries to run your page without you could run into this. See the OnlyFans Transparency Center for how verification works.

## 2. Phishing Emails And Texts

### How It Works

You get a message that looks like it's from OnlyFans, your bank or a payment provider. It says your account is locked, a payout failed, or your verification has expired. It includes a link to "fix" the problem. That link goes to a fake login page that captures your username and password.

### What The FTC Says

The FTC's guide on [recognizing and avoiding phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-avoid-phishing-scams) says scammers might "say they've noticed some suspicious activity or log-in attempts," "claim there's a problem with your account or your payment information," or "say you need to confirm some personal or financial information." It adds that "legitimate companies won't email or text with a link to update your payment information."

### How To Protect Yourself

- **Don't click.** Open onlyfans.com yourself, or use a bookmark, and check your account directly.
- **Check the sender and the link,** but remember that addresses and logos can be faked.
- **Use multi-factor authentication** wherever it's offered. The FTC says it "makes it harder for scammers to log in to your accounts if they do get your username and password."
- **Report phishing.** The FTC suggests forwarding phishing emails to reportphishing@apwg.org and reporting the attempt at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

## 3. Fake Brand Deals And Sponsorships

### How It Works

An "agency" or "brand manager" offers a paid sponsorship or collaboration. Then come the conditions, commonly reported by creators: pay for a "starter kit," pay a "registration" fee, download a file to "view the contract," or send personal and banking details before the deal is confirmed.

### Red Flags

- You have to pay something to get paid
- The contract arrives as a download or a link to log in somewhere
- They ask for ID or bank details before a written contract
- The brand's real website and social accounts don't mention the person contacting you

### How To Protect Yourself

Real sponsors pay you; you don't pay them. Contact the brand through its official website to check that the person works there. Don't download files from people you don't know. If a payment arrives for more than agreed and they ask for some back, stop. The FTC describes that [overpayment pattern](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-spot-avoid-and-report-fake-check-scams) as a scam.

## 4. Account Takeovers

### How It Works

Someone gets into your OnlyFans account, usually through a reused password, a phishing link, a hacked email account or a login you shared. Once inside, they may change your email, change your payout details or message your fans.

### Warning Signs

- Login alerts or password reset emails you didn't request
- Changes to your email address, profile or bank details that you didn't make
- Messages or posts you didn't send

### What To Do

1. **Secure your email first.** Whoever controls your email can usually reset your other passwords.
2. **Change your OnlyFans password** and turn on any extra login security available in your account settings.
3. **Check your banking and payout details** for changes.
4. **Contact OnlyFans support** through the official site, not through a link you were sent.
5. **Report it.** In the US, report scams to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you think a scammer has your financial or identity details, the FTC recommends IdentityTheft.gov.

## 5. Fake Support And Fake Verification

A message claims to be "OnlyFans support" or "verification team" and asks for your ID, a selfie, your password or a code that was sent to your phone. Creators commonly report these arriving by DM, email or social media.

Treat any unexpected request for your login, codes or ID as a scam until you've confirmed it inside your own account on the official site. Never share a one-time login code with anyone.

## Creator Security Checklist

- A **unique password** for OnlyFans and your email
- **Multi-factor authentication** on your email and any account that offers it
- **No shared logins**, including with agencies and assistants, unless you fully trust and have a written agreement
- Check your **payout and banking details** regularly
- Bookmark the **official site** and always log in from there
- A **separate email** for your creator work
- Keep **screenshots** of agency offers, contracts and suspicious messages

## Final Thoughts

Most scams aimed at OnlyFans creators want one of two things: your login, or a share of your income in exchange for promises. Keep your password and codes to yourself, go to the official site rather than clicking links, and check any agency or brand carefully before you sign or pay. Slow decisions protect your account.
