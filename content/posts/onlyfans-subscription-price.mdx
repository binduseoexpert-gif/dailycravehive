**Set your OnlyFans subscription price by defining what membership includes, calculating what remains after the platform fee and choosing a price you can sustain.** A free page removes the monthly subscription charge; it does not remove the need for a clear paid offer.

The useful question is not “What does everyone else charge?” It is “What am I asking a fan to pay for each month, and can I deliver it at this price?”

OnlyFans' Terms say creators "are solely responsible for determining any pricing applicable to Creator Interactions," and the creator sign-up steps include: "Set a monthly subscription price, if any, for your Fans." This guide turns that responsibility into a practical pricing process. It focuses on the monthly membership decision, not pricing individual photo sets, custom requests or locked messages.

## Step 1: Write The Membership Promise Before The Price

Start with a simple written description of what a subscriber receives. Include the type of content, a realistic posting commitment and any interaction you are willing to provide.

For example, a photography creator might plan a monthly theme, a behind-the-scenes post each week and a subscriber poll. That is an illustrative offer, not an OnlyFans requirement or a formula for sales.

Then separate what is not included:

- Individually commissioned work
- Optional locked-message purchases
- Rush delivery
- Personal services or access you do not offer

Do not describe a membership as “everything included” if you plan to sell additional content separately. OnlyFans' Terms define subscription access separately from pay-per-view content and require uploaded content to match its description.

Keep boundaries as clear as benefits. A monthly payment should not become an open-ended promise of unlimited replies or custom work unless that is genuinely what you offer and can deliver.

## Step 2: Decide Whether A Free Or Paid Page Fits That Offer

### Choose A Paid Model For A Defined Membership

A paid model asks a visitor to buy the subscription itself. Build a clear explanation of what that purchase covers. You should be able to explain the value without relying on a long list of future extras.

This is a business-design choice, not a claim that paid pages always earn more. A price that works for one creator does not establish demand for another.

### Choose A Free Model When Individual Purchases Are The Main Offer

With a free subscription, the membership charge is zero. OnlyFans' features guide confirms that creators can sell PPV through messages and that free accounts also have PPV functionality for posts and streams, subject to streaming eligibility.

Before choosing this model, write down the paid offer separately from the free content. Account for the time needed to create, describe and manage those offers. A large follower count alone does not pay your production costs.

Neither model guarantees sales. Pick the one whose workload and promise make sense for your own content business.

## Step 3: Do The 20% Fee Math

OnlyFans' Terms of Service (**Last updated: October 2026**) say: "Our fee for Creator Interactions is calculated as **20% of the total User Payment** and will be deducted from each User Payment." Subscriptions are one kind of Creator Interaction.

For a simple subscription-only illustration:

**Amount after platform fee = subscription payment × 0.80**

| Illustrative monthly price | Platform fee | Amount after platform fee |
|---|---|---|
| $10 | $2 | $8 |
| $15 | $3 | $12 |
| $20 | $4 | $16 |

These are calculation examples, not recommended prices, account limits or claims about typical earnings. They exclude tax, refunds, currency conversion, bank charges and production costs. For payout mechanics, see our [OnlyFans fees and payouts guide](/onlyfans-fees-payouts).

### Turn A Budget Into A Required Subscriber Count

Suppose your own monthly production budget and compensation target total $400. At an illustrative $10 subscription, the amount after OnlyFans' fee is $8 per payment. You would need **50 full-price subscription payments** to reach $400 before the other costs and adjustments listed above.

At an illustrative $20, the equivalent calculation is $400 ÷ $16 = **25 payments**.

That does not prove the higher price is better. It only shows what each scenario requires. The calculation cannot tell you how many people will subscribe, stay or renew.

If either required audience feels unrealistic for your present situation, change the workload, budget or offer rather than treating hypothetical subscribers as guaranteed income.

## Step 4: Choose A Starting Price You Can Explain

Use three checks before entering a price:

1. **Offer check:** Does the page explain the content included in the monthly charge?
2. **Workload check:** Can you keep that promise without needing every subscriber to buy extras?
3. **Budget check:** Have you accounted for the platform fee and your actual production costs?

Choose a starting price within the options offered in your account. This article does not prescribe a universal minimum, maximum or “best” price.

Do not copy a competitor's price without comparing what the memberships include. One page may sell a small archive, another an ongoing series, and another a membership with separate paid releases. The visible price is only one part of that offer.

If your problem is that few people reach the page at all, review traffic separately. Our [starting OnlyFans with no followers guide](/start-onlyfans-no-followers) covers launch and promotion rather than the monthly pricing decision.

## Step 5: Review Results Without Changing Everything At Once

OnlyFans' features guide says the Statistics page provides earnings data, view counts and other performance metrics. Use your own account records to examine the offer rather than relying on a claimed industry conversion rate.

For a practical review, record:

- The monthly price and exactly what it included
- Subscription payments received during the period
- Any discounts or other changes that affected the amount paid
- Production and administration costs
- Whether you fulfilled the promised content
- Relevant feedback about the offer

Keep subscription revenue separate from optional extra purchases. Otherwise, a good month for one-off sales can hide a membership that costs too much time to maintain.

When comparing periods, note changes in promotion, content output and audience. A price change alongside a different content plan and a large traffic spike does not tell you which change caused the result.

This is a suggested review method, not a platform benchmark. Small samples are a reason to avoid confident conclusions, not a reason to invent missing data.

## Changing Your Subscription Price: Renewal Matters

OnlyFans' Terms tell fans that a Subscription "will automatically renew at the current rate, plus any applicable Tax, for additional periods of the same duration unless you cancel it by the renewal date." The same section lists "the Subscription price has increased" among the exceptions to that renewal charge.

Do not budget as though an increased price will automatically charge every existing subscriber. Before changing a live price, read the renewal information and notices shown in your account. Explain the new offer clearly to fans instead of assuming that the price field communicates everything they need to know.

If you change what membership includes as well as the price, describe both changes. Keep a copy of the offer wording so you can check what was promised.

## Keep Subscription Pricing Separate From Extras

A recurring charge and a one-off purchase answer different questions. The subscription pays for the stated membership; an extra pays for a separately described item or interaction.

If locked messages form part of your plan, our [OnlyFans PPV messages guide](/onlyfans-ppv-messages) covers that tool. For priced extras and where to pin them, see our [OnlyFans tip menu guide](/onlyfans-tip-menu). This article does not repeat message-pricing ladders or tip-menu suggestions.

Avoid pricing your subscription so low that it only works if every member buys extras. Work out what happens when someone pays only the advertised monthly amount. That is a normal purchase, not a failure by the subscriber.
