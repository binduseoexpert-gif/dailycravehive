## The Fee: Same Cut, Different Fine Print

### Fansly: 80% To The Creator

Fansly's Help Center article "Getting Started on Fansly" says: "Creators receive 80% of revenue from subscriptions, sold media, messages, and tips. No hidden fees for payouts, processing, or currency conversion." That means Fansly keeps 20% of those revenue types. The Help Center also says Fansly protects creators from chargebacks "almost always," but it can deduct earnings if your account has an unusually high number of chargebacks, or if the money came from content that breaks its terms.

### Fanvue: 80% Standard, With Room For Promotions

Fanvue's Creator Earnings & Payouts policy says: "the standard Creator Earning Rate is 80% of gross revenue from Paid-for Services, with the remaining 20% retained by Fanvue as Platform Fees." Two things stand out:

- **Promotional rates.** The policy says Fanvue may offer a "Promotional Earning Rate," a temporary uplift for things like newly onboarded creators, and that the rate and duration are "published on the Platform" and can change at Fanvue's discretion. Some third-party guides quote a specific introductory rate. We couldn't confirm a figure on Fanvue's published pages, so check your dashboard for the rate that applies to you.
- **The old 85% rate has ended.** Fanvue's Help Centre says the 85% rate offered to creators who joined in 2022 "has come to an end" as the platform moves to its standard 80%.

Fanvue's policy also says earnings can be adjusted for refunds, chargebacks and fraud. If your dispute rate goes above 1.5% in any rolling 30-day period, the policy says Fanvue "may impose" a $50 administrative fee for each additional dispute, though it describes this as discretionary.

### What You Keep

At the standard 80% on both platforms, a $10 subscription leaves you $8.00, a $100 sale total leaves $80.00, and $1,000 in a month leaves $800.00. These are worked examples, not forecasts.

## Pending Periods: How Long Your Money Waits

**Fansly** is straightforward. Its Help Center says "after each transaction, your funds enter a 7-day pending period," and gives a precise example: earn $5 on the 13th at 1 pm and it becomes available on the 20th at 1 pm.

**Fanvue** says the standard Pending Period is seven days, but its policy adds that "this may be extended up to twenty-eight (28) days depending on factors such as account verification and user activity," decided by its fraud and risk systems and "subject to change without notice." A separate Help Centre article says that if a fan hasn't verified their email, a payment can be held for 30 days. Those two numbers don't quite line up, which is one more reason to check your own balance screen.

**What it means:** with Fansly you can plan around a fixed week. With Fanvue, allow for longer holds early on.

## Payout Timing And Fees

### Fansly Timing

Fansly's Payout Processing Times article says "most payout requests are reviewed and approved by our team within 48 hours, though often sooner." After that, "delivery typically occurs within 1–3 business days," while crypto, Paxum and Cosmopayment usually arrive "within 24 hours or less." International bank transfers and crypto are batched every three days. Another Help Center page says payouts "may take up to four (4) business days to fully process."

### Fanvue Timing

Fanvue's Help Centre says requests are "sent to your chosen payout method within 3-5 business days, depending on your chosen payout method." Its formal policy is more cautious: "We aim to initiate all valid Payout requests within ten (10) Business Days of receipt," and delays can come from fraud checks, banks or payment detail problems. If you don't get a payout, the policy says you must tell Fanvue within 30 days of the transaction or "no adjustments will be made."

### Payout Fees

- **Fansly:** its Help Center says there are no hidden fees for payouts, processing or currency conversion. Your own bank may still charge you.
- **Fanvue:** a Help Centre article says only one provider, TripleA, carries a Fanvue fee, at 1%. It's taken from the withdrawal amount when you request the payout, and you see the fee before you confirm.

### Dormant Balances

One Fanvue rule many creators miss: under its policy, if you don't request a payout or submit a required invoice for any part of your balance within 12 months, that portion can be classified as "Dormant." Fanvue says it will send reminders first, but after classification, creators in the UK, EEA and the rest of the world are deemed to waive the right to payment. US balances are handled under unclaimed property law. If you're not using a platform actively, withdraw your earnings.

## Discovery: How Do New Fans Find You?

This is where beginners without an audience should spend the most time.

**Fansly** gives you:

- **Profile tags**, which are private labels that help the algorithm understand what you create
- **Hashtags** for search and trending topics
- **A For You page.** Fansly's recommendations guide says to add a free preview to locked media you want shown there

**Fanvue** gives you:

- **A Discover feed.** The Help Centre says it is "randomised by default, so all eligible creators get a chance to be seen," with new, rapidly growing and high-performing accounts nearer the top
- **Suggested Creators**, with placement that's automatic and "based on your performance and growth," with no application
- **Intro videos**, which the Help Centre says are always guaranteed a placement in Discover
- **A discoverability toggle.** If you turn it off, fans can't find you in Discover or search, though your profile link still works

**What it means:** both offer built-in discovery, which sets them apart from platforms where you bring all your own traffic. Fanvue's randomised feed and guaranteed intro video placement are explicit about giving new accounts a chance. Fansly relies more on tags and the quality of your free previews. Neither promises results, and most creators still promote on social media.

## Tax And Paperwork

Both platforms put tax on you. Fansly's Help Center says it "does not withhold any taxes from the funds you earn" and that you're responsible for reporting them. Fanvue's policy says earnings are "paid gross of tax" and that it may collect or withhold taxes where the law requires, and may issue documents such as 1099s. If you're in the US, the IRS's [self-employed individuals tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) explains estimated quarterly payments and self-employment tax. Elsewhere, check your own country's tax authority.

## Who Should Pick Which?

**Fansly may suit you if:**

- You want to price tiers above $100 or run several tiers
- You'd like a single, fixed 7-day hold you can plan around
- You rely on tags and previews to get found
- You're comfortable submitting tax forms before your first withdrawal

**Fanvue may suit you if:**

- You like the idea of a randomised Discover feed and a guaranteed intro-video slot
- You're comfortable with the 5-content upload requirement for a first payout
- You may benefit from a promotional earning rate while you start
- You'll check your balance often enough to avoid dormancy

## Final Verdict

On fees, it's a tie at 80/20 on standard rates. The difference is in the details. Fansly gives you a fixed seven-day hold, a wider price range and tag-based discovery. Fanvue gives you a randomised Discover feed, a 1% payout fee on one provider and a few conditions to meet before your first payout. Both want you to handle your own taxes. Read the current terms on each, check your dashboard once you're in, and base your choice on the rules that matter most to you.
