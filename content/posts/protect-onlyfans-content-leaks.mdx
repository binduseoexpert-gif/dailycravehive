## What OnlyFans' Terms Say About Your Content

### You Own It

OnlyFans' Terms say: "While we do not own your Content, you grant us the right to submit notifications of infringement (including of copyright or trademark) on your behalf to any third-party that is infringing your Content." You also grant OnlyFans a licence to use your content to run the platform. The Terms add: "We will never sell your Content to other platforms."

### OnlyFans May Act, But Doesn't Have To

The same clause continues: "We are under no obligation to submit such notifications or to police infringements of your Content." OnlyFans may help, but you shouldn't rely on it alone.

### Fans Can View It, Not Redistribute It

The Terms say you authorise fans "to access and view your Content on OnlyFans for their own lawful and personal use." Reposting your content on another site goes beyond that.

### The Liability Limits

The Terms define a business user as someone who uses OnlyFans "for commercial reasons," which generally includes creators. For business users, OnlyFans says it is "not liable to you if your Content is copied, distributed, reposted elsewhere or its copyright is infringed." It is also not liable for disclosure of your identity or personal information by other users or third parties without your consent ("doxing"). In plain terms, protecting your content is mostly up to you.

### Where To Report

The Terms say OnlyFans' **DMCA Takedown Policy** covers reports "that infringing content was published on OnlyFans." Its **Complaints Policy** "explains how to make a complaint about infringement of intellectual property rights on platforms other than OnlyFans." Both are linked in the footer of OnlyFans' Terms page. The contact address in the Terms is support@onlyfans.com.

## Step 1: Prevent Leaks Where You Can

### Turn On Watermarks

In its post "How to Promote Your OnlyFans Profile," OnlyFans' blog says it can "digitally watermark your photos and videos." By default, the watermark shows your profile URL, and the blog calls watermarking "a great way to protect your copyright." Watermarks don't stop screenshots, but they make leaked content traceable and point viewers back to your page. Our guide to [starting OnlyFans with no followers](/start-onlyfans-no-followers) shows where watermarks fit into setting up a new page.

### Other Commonly Reported Habits

- **Keep identifying details out of shot,** such as your face, tattoos, house details and mail, if privacy matters to you. Our guide on [how to take feet pics](/how-to-take-feet-pics) covers keeping your identity out of frame.
- **Use teasers on social media,** not full paid content.
- **Keep originals backed up and private,** with their original files and dates, so you can prove what's yours.
- **Avoid selling content through DMs off-platform,** where it's easily resold and you lose OnlyFans' records.
- **Watch for "fans" who ask for unwatermarked files.** This is a commonly reported warning sign.

## Step 2: Find Leaks Early

Creators commonly report these checks:

- Search your creator name and handles regularly
- Run reverse image searches on your teasers
- Set alerts for your creator name
- Ask trusted fans to report leaks to you

### Keep A Leak Log

Keep a log of every leak you find: the URL, the date, a screenshot, the site's name, and which of your posts it copies. The US Copyright Office says a notice must give "information reasonably sufficient" to find the infringing material, which "often" means specific URLs. A log also helps you check later whether content was actually removed or has reappeared.

## Step 3: Send DMCA Takedown Notices

### How Notice-And-Takedown Works

In the US, Section 512 of the copyright law, added by the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, sets up what the US Copyright Office calls the "notice-and-takedown system." The Copyright Office's [Section 512 resources page](https://www.copyright.gov/512/) explains that it lets rightsholders "send a notification to the online service provider regarding infringing material." Once a provider receives a compliant notice, the Copyright Office says it "must act expeditiously to remove or disable access" to the material if it wants to keep its legal safe harbour.

### What A Notice Must Contain

According to the Copyright Office, a notice must contain "substantially" the following:

1. Your signature, or that of an authorised agent
2. Identification of the copyrighted work (or a representative list if several are on one site)
3. Identification of the infringing material and information reasonably sufficient to locate it
4. Your contact information
5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief the use isn't authorised by you, your agent or the law
6. A statement that the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you're authorised to act for the copyright owner

The Copyright Office also publishes a sample notice on the same page. If you're unsure about any element, get legal advice.

### Where To Send It

The Copyright Office says to send your notice to the site's registered DMCA agent. You can find it through the Copyright Office's DMCA Designated Agent directory, in the site's Terms of Service, or by searching the site for "DMCA." Many large sites have their own DMCA webforms, which the Copyright Office notes may be processed faster.

### Things Creators Often Get Wrong

- **You don't need to register your copyright first.** The Copyright Office says registration isn't required to send a takedown, although US works need registration before you can sue.
- **You don't need to hire anyone.** The Copyright Office says you can send a notice yourself if you own the copyright.
- **Who took the photo matters.** The Copyright Office explains that if you took the photo, such as a selfie, you are "likely the copyright owner." If someone else took it, you may not be, and other routes such as privacy laws or the site's own terms may apply.
- **Uploaders can push back.** If the uploader believes the removal was a mistake, they can file a counter-notice.

### Only File Honest Notices

Only send takedowns for content you own or are authorised to act for. The Copyright Office warns that if you "knowingly make a material misrepresentation" in a notice, you can be liable for damages, costs and attorneys' fees. For co-authored content, OnlyFans' Terms say every participant must be a tagged creator or an adult whose ID and "written, informed consent" you have, so coordinate with your co-creator before filing.

Outside the US, rules differ, so check your local copyright authority.

## Step 4: Remove Leaks From Google

Even if a site ignores you, you can ask Google to stop showing the page in its results. Google's page on [reporting content for legal reasons](https://support.google.com/legal/answer/3110420) says to select the Google product, select the reason and "include the specific URL(s) of the content you want to report, as opposed to the link to the website's home page."

Google also has a page on how to [remove personal sexual content from Google Search](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/6302812). Read the criteria carefully. Google says this route covers imagery you didn't consent to, or content you consented to that "isn't commercialized online or somewhere else." Because paid OnlyFans content is commercialized, the copyright route is usually the relevant one, and Google's page says: "If you're concerned about copyright infringement of your work, you can request removal under DMCA." The same page also covers fake sexual images of you, including ones made with AI, and search results that wrongly associate you with sexual material.

Removal from Google results doesn't delete the page from the site itself, so send the site a takedown too.

## Step 5: Consider Professional Help, Carefully

Content-protection services that file takedowns for creators are **commonly reported** to exist. You don't have to use one, as the Copyright Office notes you can file notices yourself. Before you pay one:

- Never hand over your OnlyFans login
- Avoid anyone guaranteeing "100% removal"
- Be careful with upfront fees and long contracts
- Ask exactly what they file, where, and how they report results
