## Payouts: Minimum, Timing & Methods
 
Getting paid matters as much as what you earn. Both platforms hold your money in an on-site wallet until you withdraw it.
 
### FeetFinder Payouts
 
- **Minimum:** $30 before you can withdraw.
- **Timing:** payouts run on a weekly cycle, then usually take 3 to 5 business days to reach you.
- **Methods:** US sellers are paid through Segpay to a linked bank account. International sellers are paid through Paxum, so you'll need a Paxum account set up before your first withdrawal.
- **Privacy:** the payment shows under the processor's name, not "FeetFinder", on bank statements.
**What to expect in practice:** most sellers get paid on schedule, but FeetFinder's Trustpilot page (roughly 3.5 out of 5 in mid-2026) includes complaints about delayed payouts, often tied to identity re-checks or new accounts. Keep a small buffer and don't plan bills around your first withdrawal date.
 
### Fun With Feet Payouts
 
- **Minimum:** $50 in your wallet before you can request a withdrawal.
- **Timing:** requests are usually processed within 24 to 48 hours, which is faster than FeetFinder's weekly cycle.
- **Holds:** if a payment processor flags a transaction as suspicious, the funds can be held for up to 30 days.
**The catch with the $50 floor:** if your first sales are small, say two $15 bundles, your money sits in the wallet until you cross $50. On a site with slower sales, that can take weeks. FeetFinder's $30 floor gets you to your first withdrawal sooner.
 
**Winner for fast cash-out:** Fun With Feet processes requests faster once you're over $50. FeetFinder gets you to a withdrawable balance sooner. If speed matters to you, the deciding question is how quickly you'll actually reach each floor.

## Safety, Privacy & Verification
 
Money aside, safety is where sellers can lose the most. Here's how the two compare.
 
### ID Checks
 
**FeetFinder** requires government ID from both sellers and buyers. Buyers have to be verified before they can message you, which cuts down on fake accounts, bots and people fishing for free content. It's run by FLRT Inc., a US company, and has operated since 2019.
 
**Fun With Feet** verifies sellers with ID, and it keeps that information away from buyers. Buyer checks are lighter, and the site doesn't publish much about the company behind it, such as a detailed About page or company name on its pricing information. That doesn't make it unsafe, but it gives you less to go on.
 
### Selling Without Showing Your Face
 
You can sell on both platforms without ever showing your face or using your real name. Your ID goes to the platform for verification and isn't shown to buyers. To stay anonymous in practice:
 
- Use a stage name and a separate email you don't use anywhere else.
- Crop out faces, tattoos, birthmarks and jewelry you wear often.
- Check backgrounds for mail, windows, room details or reflections.
- Strip location data (EXIF) from photos before uploading.
- Keep every conversation and payment inside the platform.

### Scams & Chargebacks
 
The most common scams in this niche are buyers asking to pay outside the platform (gift cards, Cash App, "I'll send extra"), fake "verification fee" messages, and people asking for free samples to "check quality". The rule is simple: never take payment or send content outside the platform.
 
On **chargebacks**, the two differ. FeetFinder's terms allow chargebacks and refunds to come back against a seller's balance. At least one detailed 2026 review reports that Fun With Feet absorbs chargebacks instead of passing them to sellers. If that holds, it's a real point in Fun With Feet's favor, especially for sellers doing higher-value custom work. Confirm it in the seller terms when you sign up.
