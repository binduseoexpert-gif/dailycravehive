If anyone besides you appears in a video, photo or other post, Fansly treats them as a **co-performer**. You add them on the **Co-Performers** page in your Creator Dashboard, and you **wait for approval before you publish** content that features them.

This is not the same as [your own creator verification](/fansly-verification), the Ondato check, handwritten "For Fansly" sign and application video that approve *you* as the account owner. Co-performer consent is the later step for every other person in the frame.

Fansly's help article is blunt about couples pages. Asked "Can I have a couples account?", it answers: **"No. Accounts on Fansly will have one account owner: the person who submits a creator application."** Anyone else featured in the content "is considered a co-performer" and needs consent documentation on file. The approved application's details are what Fansly uses for taxes and account recovery.

## Three Ways To Add A Co-Performer

From the Co-Performers page, click **Add Co-performer** and pick one option:

| Route | Who it's for | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Request an On-Site Fansly Creator** | Another Fansly creator | A consent request, "similar to a friend request." It replaces creator tags as proof of consent |
| **Invite Someone Not on Fansly** | Someone who won't create a Fansly account | A secure link: they verify identity with **Ondato** and e-sign consent. No account is created |
| **Add Manually** | You already hold their paperwork | You enter their details and upload the required documents yourself |

The Creator Hub says consent submitted through the old email process, whether "previously approved or pending," remains valid. The new dashboard applies to new requests going forward.

If your collaborator isn't a Fansly creator yet, Fansly calls it "the simplest verification method" to encourage them to apply, then send an on-site request. Their application is the normal owner verification; this guide stays on the co-performer record.

Fansly recommends on-site requests "whenever possible" to avoid delays.

## Route 1: On-Site Requests

1. Go to **Creator Dashboard > Co-Performers**.
2. Click **Add Co-Performer** and choose **Request an On-Site Fansly Creator**.
3. Send the request through Fansly.
4. Once accepted, they appear in your Co-Performers dashboard.

You manage requests on the **Requests** tab (fansly.com/creator/coperformers/requests):

- Incoming requests show a **blue circle** next to Co-performers on the Creator Dashboard. The circle may be **yellow** if the Compliance team also has action requests waiting for you.
- The on-site request "will only show for **7 days**, after that it will not be reflected." If your collaborator misses it, send a new one.
- For each request you can **Accept** or **Decline**. Accepted requests appear under **Creators Featuring You**.
- Filter for **Sent** to check requests you made. Declined or revoked requests appear there too.

Even with an accepted on-site request, the Creator Hub says you are "still legally required to keep consent documentation on file."

## Route 2: Invite Someone Who Isn't On Fansly

If your co-performer can't or won't create an account, generate a **secure link**. The **label** is how they appear in your list, so use a name or nickname you'll recognise later. You can add a personal message; they'll see it along with your username when they open the link.

Through **Ondato**, the verification provider Fansly partners with, they:

- Complete identity verification
- Electronically sign the consent documents

Fansly says this "does not create a Fansly account or approve them as a creator." The Ondato completion request "is only valid for **14 days**." After that, you need to send another invite.

Once they finish, the submission appears on your dashboard with their first name, selfie and age.

## Route 3: Manual Submissions

Use this when you upload the paperwork yourself:

1. Enter the person's information. The **Label** field controls how they appear in your dashboard.
2. Click **Continue**.
3. Upload the required supporting documents.
4. Click **Submit for Review**.

The Creator Hub lists what a manual submission needs:

- A signed Fansly consent form
- A clear photo of their government-issued ID
- A clear photo of their signed, legible consent form
- A photo of them holding their ID next to the signed consent form

Fansly's help article links its official "Consent to Recording and Publication" form for manual submissions. It also notes that the form now has **required initials at the bottom of each page**, which must be completed along with the signatures.

### Original Files, Not Copies

Fansly's wording: "We keep these documents on file as official records, so we need them exactly as they were created. Please don't upload scans, photos, or screenshots of a document you already have."

- **E-signed forms** (DocuSign, Dropbox Sign, Adobe Sign and similar): download the completed PDF from the signing service or its confirmation email, then upload that file.
- **ID photos and selfies:** upload directly from the device that took them, uncropped and unedited.
- "Copies and screenshots usually can't be accepted, and submitting them will delay your review."

### The Tying Photo

A **tying photo** shows the co-performer holding both their government-issued ID and the signed consent form. It must clearly show their face, the ID and the page with their signature. The first page of the form doesn't need to be visible, but the completed form must still be uploaded.

The Help Center calls the tying photo **optional** at first but **strongly recommended**: submissions can be reviewed without one, but it "may help prevent delays or rejections." If you don't have it yet, upload it as soon as possible.

**One official conflict to know about:** the Creator Hub's manual checklist lists "a photo of them holding their ID next to the signed consent form" as something you'll need, while the Help Center article says the tying photo is optional at first. We'd treat it as required in practice and include it from the start.

### Accuracy, Drafts And Review Time

Check every detail before you submit. Fansly says you "will not be able to change these once the request is submitted," and inaccurate information may mean a new submission and a delayed approval. Review "can take up to 7 days."

- **Approved:** you'll see an **Approved** status next to the submission.
- **More information needed:** the request comes back with notes. Use the **Action Needed** filter to find it.
- **Drafts:** if you save a draft and come back, Date of Birth and ID number won't be displayed. A green check mark means they were saved.

Each collaborator needs **separate** documentation. If several people are involved, submit for each person individually; "group photos are not accepted."

## State Rules: Alabama And North Carolina

**Alabama:** Fansly says Alabama residents "must have all consent forms notarized as per state law." The Alabama consent form includes a notary section on the third page. Upload all three completed pages, including the notary acknowledgement and the required initials. For the tying photo, only the last page with the co-performer's signature needs to be visible.

**North Carolina:** Fansly's HB 805 article says creators residing in North Carolina "are prohibited from uploading content featuring co-performers," whether or not the co-performer is a Fansly creator or has consent forms on file. "Prior guidelines that permitted co-performer content with proper consent no longer apply to North Carolina residents." Fansly says it cannot offer a workaround; a legislative change in North Carolina would be required.

A few more points from the same article:

- Fansly decides who counts as a North Carolina resident from your **account address** and other residency information you've submitted.
- Solo content is still allowed. North Carolina creators also see a state consent form during upload for qualifying content, and it has to be completed **for each upload**.
- If you've moved, update your personal information so your account address is current.

State laws change. Re-check the official articles before you shoot or upload.

## Tagging Is Not Consent

Older tagged collaborations "remain valid," but Fansly asks you to use **on-site requests** for new collaborations, and says: "Eventually, tagging will no longer be accepted as a form of consent."

Its capitalised warning ends: "TAGGING ≠ CONSENT. IT JUST MEANS WE DIDN'T NEED THE PAPERWORK. YOU STILL DO." If Fansly ever needs to verify consent and you can't provide it, it says that's on you, not your collaborator.

For anyone who is not a verified creator, "signed consent documents are mandatory. No exceptions."

**Reposting is different.** The Creator Hub says hitting the repost icon on another creator's post shares it with full credit "without needing a consent form." **Re-uploading** that content to your own account needs a signed consent form from everyone involved.

## After Approval, And If Consent Is Withdrawn

Publish **only after** the record shows Approved. To check whether you've already submitted someone, search and filter the Co-Performers page by status (Approved, Action Needed and so on) or submission type (Manual, On-site, External). For on-site requests you can search by username or display name. Documents you sent by email in the past may not show up yet; Fansly says its team is adding them.

To withdraw consent:

- **Accepted on-site request:** use the red **Revoke** button on the Requests tab. Fansly says this "will only apply to content moving forward" and "will not impact any previous content."
- **Earlier media, or other cases:** follow the "Retracting Consent for Shared Content" process. Contact the other creator first, then fill out the official Fansly Revocation of Consent Form and email it to compliance@fansly.com with a clear photo of your government-issued ID and a photo of yourself holding the signed form and your ID.

## Final Thoughts

Co-performer consent on Fansly is a dashboard workflow, not a tag and not a second login on your account. Add every other person before you publish, pick the route that fits (on-site request, secure invite or manual upload), use original files when you go manual, and wait for Approved. Keep your own records even when Fansly holds a copy. State rules, such as Alabama's notarization and North Carolina's co-performer ban, sit on top of the platform process, so re-check the official help articles before you post.
