## What OnlyFans Officially Says

The OnlyFans Creator Center has a short section titled "Block by Country." It says creators "have the option to block users in specific states, and even entire countries, from accessing your profile." It adds that this "can be especially helpful for creators who want to maintain their privacy in certain geographical areas."

To use it, the Creator Center says to "enable Block By Country in your privacy and security settings."

That's the extent of the official explanation we could load. OnlyFans doesn't publish a detailed technical description of how it detects a visitor's location. Several creator sites quote an OnlyFans Help Center article that adds more detail about IP addresses and which countries support state-level blocking. We couldn't open that help page directly, so those details are labelled **commonly reported** below.

## How To Block A Country Or State On OnlyFans

The steps below combine the official instruction with the menu path creators commonly report. Labels can vary between the app, mobile web and desktop.

1. **Log in and open Settings.** Use the profile menu on your OnlyFans account.
2. **Go to Privacy and safety.** The Creator Center calls this your "privacy and security settings." On the site the menu is commonly reported as "Privacy and safety."
3. **Find the Safety section.** Creators commonly report the geoblocking controls sit near the bottom, under a heading like "IP and Geo Blocking."
4. **Choose By Country.** Select every country you want to block.
5. **Choose By State if needed.** OnlyFans confirms state-level blocking exists. Creators commonly report it's available for the United States, Canada and Australia.
6. **Save, then reload.** Reopen the page to make sure your selections stuck.

Creators also commonly report a **By IP address** option. It's only useful in narrow cases, because home and mobile IP addresses often change and can be shared by many people.

## Which Places Should You Block?

There's no single right answer. It's a trade-off between privacy and reach. Every place you block is a place where potential fans can't see your page. Creators commonly approach it like this:

- **Your home state or province.** This is the most common choice when the country is too big to block entirely but local people are the main worry.
- **Where family lives.** If your parents or relatives live in another state or country, block that area too.
- **Your home country.** Some creators block their whole country, especially in smaller countries where people are more likely to know each other. Others decide the loss of fans isn't worth it.
- **Where you work or study.** If your workplace or campus is in a different state, consider adding it.

You can use the **Reach** tab on your Statistics page to make a more informed choice. The Creator Center says it lets you "find out how many people view your profile, what countries they're from, and how much time they spend looking at your content." That tells you roughly how much traffic a block might cost you.

## What Geoblocking Can't Do

Geoblocking is helpful, but it's not an invisibility cloak. Knowing its limits will stop you from relying on it too much.

### It Depends On Location Detection

Websites generally estimate a visitor's location from their IP address. This is called [internet geolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_geolocation), and it's never perfectly accurate. Anyone whose connection appears to come from somewhere else may not be blocked. That includes someone who is travelling, or someone using a VPN or proxy service, who may appear to be somewhere else. It also works the other way: if location detection is wrong, a genuine fan outside the blocked area could be blocked by mistake. [Geo-blocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geo-blocking) has the same limitation everywhere it's used, from streaming services to online shops.

So treat geoblocking as a strong filter against **casual discovery**, not a guarantee against someone who is actively looking for you.

### It Doesn't Cover Content Outside OnlyFans

Geoblocking only controls who can open your OnlyFans profile. It doesn't hide:

- Your promotional posts on social media, Reddit or link-in-bio pages
- Content that has been leaked or reposted on other sites
- Search results that show your stage name or photos

If your content has already appeared elsewhere, see our guide to [protecting OnlyFans content from leaks](/protect-onlyfans-content-leaks). It covers watermarks, DMCA takedowns and removing leaks from Google.

### It Doesn't Hide Your Face Or Identity

If someone who knows you sees your face, tattoos or a distinctive room on any platform, a block won't help. Many creators combine geoblocking with careful framing, a separate stage name and a separate email address.

## Block Specific People Too

Geoblocking hides your page from places. To stop a known individual, OnlyFans offers two separate tools, according to the Creator Center:

- **Restrict:** a restricted subscriber "will still have access to your profile, but their messages and comments to you will be hidden." The Creator Center compares it to muting someone.
- **Block:** "Once blocked, a user will have no way of accessing your profile or communicating with you on the platform in any way."

To use either, the Creator Center says to click the three dots in the top right corner of one of the user's messages, posts or profile. You can restrict or block subscribers, but you can only block non-subscribers.

If you know a specific ex or harasser has an OnlyFans account, blocking that account works alongside geoblocking their area. Report threats or abuse with the report button that, according to the Creator Center, appears on each image, video, post, message and account on OnlyFans.

## A Simple Privacy Checklist

Geoblocking works best as part of a routine. Here's a checklist built from OnlyFans' official guidance and commonly reported creator practice:

1. **Turn on Block by Country** for your home area and anywhere family lives.
2. **Block known accounts** of people you don't want seeing your page.
3. **Turn on watermarking.** The Creator Center says you can turn on automatic digital watermarking "at any time from the Privacy and safety menu in your profile Settings."
4. **Never share identifying details.** The Creator Center says that if you share content or information which compromises your safety, "such as a physical address," OnlyFans will remove it.
5. **Use a separate email and stage name** for your creator accounts (commonly recommended).
6. **Check what Google shows about you.** Google's ["Results about you"](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076) tool helps you find and request removal of search results that show personal info like your home address, phone number or email address.
7. **Be careful with promotion.** Promotional accounts are visible everywhere, regardless of your OnlyFans settings. Our guide to [starting OnlyFans with no followers](/start-onlyfans-no-followers) covers promotion basics.
8. **Recheck after changes.** Review your settings after app updates, a move, or if anyone else ever had access to your account.

Also watch out for people who offer to "unblock" or "hide" your account for a fee, or ask for your login to "fix" your privacy settings.
