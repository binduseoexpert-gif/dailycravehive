## Clock Calendar Vs Trash-Can Calendar

Fansly's scheduling article opens with a heads-up: when you compose a new post, you'll see **two calendar icons**.

- **The one with a clock** is for scheduling posts: when the post should **go live**.
- **The one with a trash can** is for setting an **expiration date**: when a live post should **come down**.

Mixing them up is the easiest mistake in this workflow. Scheduling delays publication. Expiration removes a post later. Official help treats them as separate tools, with a separate "Scheduling Posts to Expire" article for the trash-can icon.

The "Posting" article lists both as composer options: **Schedule Post** ("plan your posts ahead of time and set them to go live whenever you want") and **Post Expiration** ("perfect for limited-time offers or special events"). It does not spell out how the two settings interact if you use both on the same post, so confirm what the composer shows before you assume a combined result.

| Icon (official description) | What it controls | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar **with a clock** | Publish date and time | You want the post to go live later, automatically |
| Calendar **with a trash can** | Expiration date and time | You want a post to come down automatically at a set time |

## How To Schedule A Post

From Fansly's "Post Scheduling" article:

1. **Open the post composer.** On desktop, it's on the **home screen**. On mobile, tap the **+** at the bottom. The "Posting" article says the same: desktop composer at the top of your homepage; mobile **+** at the bottom of the screen.
2. Click the **calendar with a clock** icon.
3. **Choose the date and time** for your post.
4. Click **Confirm Date**.
5. **Create the rest of the post as usual.**

After that, "your post will now be queued to publish automatically at your chosen time."

Official help does not say which timezone the date-and-time picker uses. Check the time displayed in the composer and, once it's queued, in the Scheduled Post Queue, before you treat it as a local-clock promise.

### What "The Rest Of The Post As Usual" Includes

Scheduling does not skip the usual composer work. The "Posting" article describes those options: a caption (including markdown), media from your device or Vault, media permissions, optional lock text, polls, tip goals, pin-to-walls, and whether the post goes to the For You Page.

Set **media permissions** before the post is queued if the files shouldn't be open to everyone. A scheduled post that goes live with the wrong lock is still a permissions problem. ## How To Manage The Scheduled Post Queue

Official help: click your **profile picture**, then select **Scheduled Post Queue**. "Here you'll see all your upcoming scheduled posts."

From a queued post you can:

- **Open it** to review details
- **Make edits**
- **Adjust the scheduled time**
- **Remove it** if you decide not to publish, using the **trash can** icon in the queue

That trash can is not the expiration calendar in the composer. In the queue, official help says it deletes the scheduled post so it will not publish. This is about taking a draft out of the queue, not closing your Fansly account.

Official help does not publish a cap on how many posts you can have in the queue, or a latest-possible schedule date. Don't assume a limit until the current queue or composer shows one.

| Queue action (official) | Result |
|---|---|
| Review details | Check the post as queued |
| Make edits | Change the post before it goes live |
| Adjust the scheduled time | Change when it will publish |
| Trash can in the queue | The scheduled post will not publish |

## Expiration Is A Different Setting

If you meant to take a post down later, that's the **trash-can calendar**, not the clock.

From "Scheduling Posts to Expire" (updated November 10, 2026):

1. Go to **fansly.com/home**.
2. In the posting menu, click the **calendar icon with the trash can**.
3. Choose the time and date you'd like your post to expire.
4. Select your media, text and permissions as usual.

"Once scheduled, your post will automatically expire at the set time, so you don't need to manually remove it later." Fansly calls this "ideal for limited-time content."

Use expiration for offers that should vanish on a deadline. Use the clock calendar when the post shouldn't appear until a deadline. If you only needed a later publish time and you set expiration instead, the post may go live straight away and then disappear at the set time, the opposite of a queue.

## What Official Help Does Not Promise

Fansly says scheduling is useful for planning ahead, keeping a consistent posting rhythm, and "publishing when your audience is most active." That is the platform's list of uses, not a recommended hour of the day and not a guarantee of more sales.

Official help also does not state:

- The timezone shown in the picker
- A maximum number of queued posts
- Whether a scheduled post sends fans an automatic reminder (OnlyFans' live **Queue** is a different tool, covered in our OnlyFans live-stream guide, not a Fansly feed setting)
- Best times to post, or For You Page ranking effects

Don't build a launch around any of those missing details. Confirm the queued date and time in Scheduled Post Queue, and keep promotion habits in the promotion guide.
