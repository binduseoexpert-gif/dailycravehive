## Why Use A Session Instead Of A Password

Fansly lists three reasons to create a Management Session:

- Give trusted people **controlled** access to your account
- **Decide exactly** what actions they can take
- Keep your **most sensitive actions** secure while you collaborate

The last point is the one people skip. A shared password usually means a shared inbox, a shared 2FA code and, too often, a path to payout details. Fansly says payouts and account closure stay restricted on a session. That does not make a stranger safe. It does mean you don't have to hand over the keys you would use to empty the account.

## How To Create A Management Session

From Fansly's official steps:

1. In your **Creator Dashboard**, open the **Management** page.
2. Click **Create Session**.
3. Give the session a **clear name**, so you know who it's for (Fansly's examples: "Photo Editor" or "Agency A").
4. **Choose the level of access** you want to grant. Fansly says you'll see plenty of options, but payouts and account closure are always restricted.
5. **Review the disclaimer**, then click **Create**.

Fansly adds: "Each link can allow different permissions: you can decide exactly what someone can and cannot do." Use a separate session for each person, with only the access that person needs - an editor does not need the same toggles as a chatter.

You can "create multiple links at once and then share them as needed."

Fansly does not publish a complete checkbox list in the help article. Don't assume a permission exists until you see it in the Create Session screen.

## How Your Manager Signs In

1. Share the link with them **directly**.
2. They paste it in their browser or click it to log in.
3. The link is **single-use** and uses the permissions you set.

If the manager **logs out**, or the link **expires**, they need a **new** session link from you. Fansly's wording: "This ensures no one keeps access longer than you intend."

**Practical habits (editorial, not Fansly quotes):**

- Send the link in a channel you already trust, not in a public chat or a first-time DM from a new "agency."
- Don't post the link in a screenshot or story.
- When the work is done, revoke the session even if they say they logged out.

## Edit Or Revoke A Session

You stay in control after the link is shared.

**To change permissions:** go back to the Management page, click the **pencil** icon, and enter your **2FA** code. Then adjust permissions.

**To take access away:** click the **trash can** icon next to the session and confirm **Yes**. Fansly describes this as revoking access. It is not the same as closing your Fansly account. Payouts and account closure stay off-limits to managers.

Fansly also says: "Make sure to clear out any sessions once your manager no longer needs access."

## What You're Responsible For

The official "Important Notes":

- "By creating a Management session, you acknowledge that you **are responsible for everything someone does** using your management session."
- "**Fansly Support will NEVER ask for** your management session, your 2FA, or your password!"
- Clear out sessions you no longer need.

If a session is used to message fans, change prices or post content you didn't approve, Fansly still treats that as your account. Read the disclaimer in the create flow before you click Create.

## Keep The Account Secure While You Collaborate

Fansly's "Securing Your Account" list:

- Regularly check your **Session Management** page and close any sessions you don't recognise.
- Set up **two-factor authentication (2FA)**.
- **Never share your 2FA code.**
- **Remove and reset 2FA** if you've ever shared your codes.
- **Change your password** regularly.

If you previously shared a password or 2FA codes with a helper, do those reset steps *before* you issue a Management Session. A session on top of a leaked login is not a fix.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Create a named session with the minimum access they need | Share your password so they can "just log in" |
| Share the single-use link directly | Post the link, or send it to an unverified "agency" |
| Revoke the session when the work ends or they log out | Leave old sessions sitting on the Management page |
| Keep 2FA on, and never share the code | Text your 2FA code to "support" or a manager |
| Check the Session Management page for sessions you don't recognise | Assume a logout on their side closed every door |
