## Fees: Rising, Spotlight And Icon

Selling requires an **active creator plan**. Footly's pricing page and Seller Agreement publish the same tiered platform fee:

| Plan | Monthly | Yearly | Platform fee | Creator keeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rising | $3.99 | $24.99 | **15%** | **85%** |
| Spotlight | $6.99 | $39.99 | **10%** | **90%** |
| Icon | $9.99 | $59.99 | **5%** | **95%** |

The Seller Agreement states that Footly "retains a platform fee on each sale determined by your plan tier — Rising 15% (you keep 85%), Spotlight 10% (you keep 90%), Icon 5% (you keep 95%)" and that "the same fee applies uniformly to subscriptions, pay-per-view, tips, message unlocks, and custom requests." Moving up to Icon is described on the pricing page as self-service from creator settings.

Footly's own $1,000/month sales example on the pricing page:

| Tier | Monthly cost | Platform fee on $1,000 | Net on $1,000 (after plan fee) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rising | $3.99 | $150 | $846.01 |
| Spotlight | $6.99 | $100 | $893.01 |
| Icon | $9.99 | $50 | $940.01 |

So the headline "keep up to 95%" is true **on Icon**, not on Rising. New creators who start on Rising pay the highest percentage, plus a small monthly (or yearly) plan cost on top. At low sales volume the plan fee matters more: on $50 of monthly sales, Rising's $3.99 is worth roughly another 8% of what you made. At higher volume, the percentage is what moves your take-home.

### Processing Hold Before You Can Withdraw

New earnings first appear in **Processing**. The payouts guide says that **seven days** after a payment completes, unreversed earnings move to your available balance (Footly frames this as chargeback protection). Withdrawal requests are then reviewed for security before ACH or Paxum starts. Processing, reserved and available balances are shown separately in the creator wallet.

That means a sale today is not withdrawable today. Plan cash flow around the seven-day processing period plus the weekly payout cycle — realistically one to two weeks from first sale to money in your bank.
